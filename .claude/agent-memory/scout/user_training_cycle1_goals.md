## Athlete profile (as of 2026-08)
- Level: flashes 6a/6b on Tension Board 2. Traverses ~3 min continuous. Has NEVER hangboarded.
- Stated fear: leading. "Scared leading" is the #1 before→after target — treat as the priority goal.
- Plan values (their words): achievable, simple, adaptable to life, measurable. They explicitly
  reference Steve Bechtel's *Logical Progression* — nonlinear/undulating periodization.
- Equipment: gyms w/ lead + top-rope + auto-belay, commercial boulders, TB2, spray walls,
  Moonboard, Kilterboard, **fixed gym hangboards (standard 20mm edge, maybe campus rungs)**.
  Personal: **Tindeq force gauge**, bands, portable hangboard.
- Confirmed logistics: **4 training days/week**, lead access ~1x/week, 4-week cycle (3 load + 1
  deload), session length flexible (no hard cap).
- Their draft session menu: Endurance / Limit Bouldering (boards+spray) / Hard Lead / Strength+Stretch.

## Before → after targets (cycle 1 reframes in parens)
- Scared leading → confident leading (metric = fall-ladder step, NOT grade)
- Flash 6a/6b TB2 → flash 7a TB2 (NOT a 4-week goal; ~9-18 months. Cycle 1 = flash 6c / redpoint 7a in ≤3 sessions)
- Traverse 3 min → 6 min (achievable in one 4-week cycle; the most winnable goal)
- No hangboarding → crimp + drag (density/submax only in cycle 1; NO max hangs)

## Headline calls I made (don't re-litigate)
1. **Confirmed their suspected gap**: "scared → confident leading" requires a deliberate FALL
   BLOCK + easy-lead mileage. A "hard lead session" alone trains avoidance. Prescribed a 5-step
   fall ladder, 3-5 falls EVERY lead session, placed after warmup while fresh (never fatigued),
   with a human belayer (auto-belay cannot train this — it's mileage only).
2. **No max hangs in cycle 1.** Density hangs (already in catalog, 20mm, 4x30s, submax RPE 6-7) +
   Emil submax isometrics. NO CAMPUSING in cycle 1 even though gym rungs exist.
3. **Two-instrument split (the key hangboard call).** The FIXED GYM 20mm edge is the training
   stimulus — same edge every week is what makes progress comparable. The **Tindeq is the
   measuring instrument, not the main trainer**: one-arm max isometric pull on a 20mm edge,
   half-crimp and drag separately, best of 3x5s, tested week 1 and week 4 only (never weekly —
   a max pull is its own stimulus and weekly noise swamps the signal). Record peak kg AND %BW.
   Portable home board = warmup/backup when the gym board is busy, and habit-keeper in deload.
   Load target for density hangs ≈ 55-70% of (2 x one-arm peak); for a beginner this usually means
   BAND-ASSISTED hangs (they own bands) — subtracting weight is the correct start, not a failure.
4. **Hangboard rides along on climbing days, twice a week**: loaded density hangs first-after-warmup
   on the board/power day and on the strength day. Never its own day, never after climbing in
   cycle 1 (grip-position quality degrades with fatigue, and sloppy half-crimp under fatigue is
   the pulley-injury mechanism). Whatever quality you're building goes FIRST in the session.
5. **Endurance stays LOW intensity** (ARC/continuous) while the 3→6 min goal is live. No 4x4 /
   PE intervals in cycle 1 — mixing PE with limit board is the classic interference mistake.
6. **Keep TB2 at ONE angle for the whole cycle** — changing angle destroys grade comparability,
   which breaks their "measurable" value.
7. Drill-to-day: one drill per session, inside the warmup ladder, never on limit attempts.
   Lead→breathing (fear lever). Endurance→rhythm (pacing is the limiter). Board→dynamic/deadpoint.
   Feet-first = the floating/4th-day drill and the substitute when a primary goes stale.
8. Progression metric, ONE per session type: Endurance = longest unbroken time on wall (min:sec).
   Board = hardest TB2 grade sent in ≤3 attempts. Lead = fall-ladder step reached.
   Strength = load (kg) at the fixed density-hang protocol.

## Blocking dependency I flagged
Fall practice needs a PARTNER. If they mostly climb alone on auto-belay, the entire confidence
plan changes shape — confirm belayer availability before committing to the lead day.
