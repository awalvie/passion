Researched 2026-08 for the owner's strength day. Deep pass, ~55 sources. This is the STRENGTH
counterpart to `reference_skill_acquisition_evidence.md`. Never re-research; never overclaim.

## The single most important finding for THIS athlete
**Stien et al. 2021, PLOS One (n=57 male lead climbers, 3 tiers):** elite (IRCRA 24-27) beat everyone
on peak force (ES 1.77-1.78, p<0.001), BUT **intermediate (10-17) vs advanced (18-23) were
statistically indistinguishable on EVERY strength and RFD measure (p = 0.898-1.000)**. Authors:
"the advancement from intermediate to advanced may depend on factors other than strength-based
measures." https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0249353
=> For a 6b→6c→7a climber, **strength is not the discriminator**. Strength gates at the ELITE
boundary, not through the middle of the range. Combined with Morenas 2021 from the skill memory
(watching a real climber: 6c flash 18% → 61%), the strength day is the LEAST leveraged of four days.
Also: Stien — "RFD may not be crucial before reaching demanding grades (>V9)". No campusing needed.

## What survives scrutiny
- **Finger strength is the top correlate of grade.** Faggian et al. 2024, *J Sport Health Sci*
  14:100974, **74 studies, 2,691 climbers**: finger flexor strength w/ arms extended r = 0.69-0.82
  (range across studies 0.42-0.92). Anthropometrics explain only **0.3-4.0%** of variance.
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11904605/
  Berta/Baláš 2025 (*J Sports Sci*, **n=307** — largest sample): finger strength explained **68% of
  variance in males, 64% in females**; best single predictor was the **finger hang test** (R²=0.56),
  i.e. a strength-ENDURANCE hybrid beat pure MVC.
- **Climbing + resistance training > climbing alone.** Stien et al. 2023, *Biol Sport* 40(1):179-191,
  meta-analysis, 5 studies / 110 climbers: overall climbing-specific tests **SMD 0.57 (0.24-0.91)**;
  finger strength 0.41 (0.03-0.80, CI nearly touches zero); RFD 0.91; dead-hang endurance 1.23.
  I²=0%. But PEDro median 6/10, **no blinding anywhere**, 4-10 weeks.
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9806751/
- **Semi-specific beats both specific and unspecific.** Langer, Simon & Wiemeyer 2023, *JSCR*
  37(3):751-767, 12 studies: "a preference for **semispecific** exercises over highly specific or
  unspecific approaches"; combine static + dynamic. Hangboard/campus = semi-specific; barbell =
  unspecific. https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/en/works/4L6VeAL9/
- **Previous injury is the strongest injury predictor.** Jones 2015: reinjury RR **1.55 (1.34-1.80)**;
  overuse-reinjury probability **63.0%**. Load management, not exercise selection.
- **Fingers dominate injury** at every level: 41.3% of chronic injuries (Grønhaug 2018, n=667);
  61.3% in adolescents (Schöffl 2025). Shoulders 19.4%, elbows 17.7%.

## What does NOT survive — say this plainly
- **That training finger strength improves CLIMBING.** Saeterbakken et al. 2024, *Front Physiol*
  15:1461820, RCT n=31 advanced/elite: 1RM finger strength **+11.5% vs +5.3%, p=0.021, ES 1.83** —
  intervention clearly worked — but **bouldering performance (Kilter) p = 0.39** (DFS +3.8%, CON
  -2.5%). Isometric peak force p=0.56, RFD p=0.30.
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11499159/
  Hermans 2022 (n=35, 10wk): peak force +89.7N, ES 1.12, between-group p=0.008 — but no climbing
  test at all, and authors say so. **The two trials that measured performance vs a control found
  nothing.** This is the central contradiction of the field.
- **Full crimp as a priority grip.** Faggian: full crimp is the **WEAKEST** predictor of the grips
  tested (r = 0.47-0.58) vs slope 0.67-0.72, half crimp 0.60-0.65. Quarmby 2023 lists **crimp grip
  use** as an identified injury risk factor. Lattice's own training manual says **"NO Full Crimp"**.
  Hooper's Beta: "avoid full crimping as a beginner"; King & Lien 2017 — full crimp loads A2 at
  **287N of a ~400N failure load (~72%)**.
- **The half-crimp-is-your-strong-grip assumption.** Zihlmann et al. 2025, *CISS* 10(1):005, n=85:
  **open hand produced slightly HIGHER force than half crimp at every skill level**; advanced
  climbers **overestimated** their half crimp by 9.8%. https://ciss-journal.org/article/view/10986
- **Maximal pulling strength.** Faggian: **1RM weighted pull-up — no significant correlation** with
  climbing ability. But pull-up REPS r = 0.73-0.77 and **bent-arm hang r = 0.70-0.80** DO correlate.
  Bench press and squat jump **do not discriminate** climbing levels. => Lock-offs and bent-arm
  isometrics > weighted pull-ups. Buraas 2025 (n=19): pull-up 1RM r=0.55 bouldering, **ns redpoint**,
  while finger strength r=0.89.
- **Any barbell prescription.** **Zero studies** on deadlift, squat or overhead press and climbing.
  Not weak evidence — none.
- **Antagonist/pressing training for injury prevention or performance.** No study. Quarmby et al.
  2023 (**34 studies, 10,049 participants**) found no antagonist or imbalance studies in climbing at
  all. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10756908/
  Its NON-findings are as useful: warm-up/cool-down, static stretching, and taping are all **NOT**
  protective; BMI/bodyweight not associated with injury.
  The only positive climbing prevention RCT is **Kozin et al. 2021/2022, n=84 male students aged
  18-19**, and the intervention was **closed-chain + eccentric + PNF, not pressing**; shoulder/elbow
  only; collected **no finger data**. Hampton and Corsaro both publicly reject it as too weak.
  Handball parallel is a coin-flip: Andersson 2017 cluster RCT (660 players) **worked**; Achenbach
  2022 cluster RCT (579 players) **null** — and the null one used light bands and ignored load.
- **Flexor/extensor balance & rice bucket.** Devise et al. 2023, *Front Sports Act Living*
  5:1243354 (78 assessed, 52 randomised): **"no proof of links between flexor-to-extensor balance,
  injuries and finger strength has been found"**; the link "remains unsubstantiated." And the ratio
  **correlated POSITIVELY with grade** — better climbers are more "imbalanced."
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10701375/
- **Core training improving climbing.** Saeterbakken 2018, *PLOS One* 13(10):e0203766, RCT n=18,
  isometric vs dynamic core, 10wk: **no between-group difference (p=0.300-0.926)**; neither group
  improved the body lock-off; **climbing performance never measured**. Faggian ranks core #4, plank
  discriminating only lower-to-intermediate. Pallof/body-saw: zero climbing literature.
- **Bechtel's hormonal mechanism** — see the dedicated section below.

## Dose-response — the numbers worth keeping
- **Hörst's hard ceiling (the most quotable volume rule anywhere):** "Resting the fingers a minimum
  of three days per week is essential… **Aggregate number of rigorous climbing and finger training
  days per week must not exceed four.**" https://trainingforclimbing.com/
- **Climbing should be 75-85% of training time** — independent convergence: Steven Low "80-85%+",
  Hooper's Beta "75-80%", Natasha Barnes "75/25 rule", Hampton "~15% lifting". Most robust
  consensus number in the whole body of material.
- **Lattice** (Beacon training manual, verified): max strength = 6 × 10s @85% of max total load,
  2min rest; anaerobic capacity 6 sets of 5×(7on/3off) @80%; density 30-70%, 30-60s reps, 4-8 sets.
  Progression rule: **"Never adjust weight by more than a couple of pounds per week"** and never
  adjust before completing a session successfully twice in a row.
  Deload: **3-4 week mesocycles, 2:1 or 3:1 train:rest, "reduce overall training load by 40-60%"**
  (matches Bechtel's "half volume"). Minimum effective dose: "**a single, high-quality,
  high-intensity finger strength session per week can comfortably maintain** maximum recruitment."
  No-hang stance: Lattice have **replaced fingerboard with lifting-edge workouts in >30% of plans**.
- **Lattice grade bands (n=6,000+, two-arm ADDED load, men):** V1-V4 +19.0kg · V5-V7 +24.2kg ·
  V8-V11 +38.4kg · V12+ +52.9kg. Women V1-V4 +6.6kg, V12+ +29.5kg.
  **6c boulder ≈ V5, 7a ≈ V6-7 → target band is +24.2kg two-arm.** They deliberately publish BANDS,
  not per-grade targets, and concede "one 9a climber… was not necessarily producing the same finger
  strength metrics as another." The %BW-per-V-grade table circulating online is a **third party's
  reverse-engineering** of their YouTube figures (one-arm, men only, interpolated) — NOT Lattice data.
- **Devise 2022** (*Front Sports Act Living* 4:862782, n=54, 4 groups incl. true control): F100 (6s
  @100%) +12.4% max strength; **F80 (10s @80%, 6s rest) was the ONLY protocol to improve all three
  of max strength, stamina and endurance**; control +0.7% ns. => sub-max ~80% is the best all-rounder.
- **Gilmore & Baar 2024** (*Sports Med Open* 10:125, **retrospective Crimpd app logs, n=526**, NOT an
  RCT): climbing only **no change (p>0.99)**; Abrahangs only +2.5% (p=0.12, ns); Max Hangs only
  +3.2% (p=0.0005); **both +5.8% (p<0.01 vs either alone)** — additive. The "low-intensity is as
  good as max" headline rests on a **non-significant p=0.12**, and the tendon mechanism was never
  measured.
- **General strength for a 4-day climber: 2 sessions/week of 15-45 min** is the near-universal
  answer (Hörst 2, Lattice 1-2 × 45min, Low 2 × 15-20min, Barnes 1-3, Nelson 2). Disagreement is
  only on PLACEMENT: Hörst and Low say bolt onto hard climbing days; Lattice's podcast says
  non-climbing days; Nelson says either but 4-6h apart.
- **Isometric joint-angle carryover is only ±10°** (Lum & Barbosa 2019, *Int J Sports Med*
  40:363-375). So straight-arm / bent-arm / lock-off ARE three distinct stimuli — and so are the
  three grip positions. Splitting a fixed finger budget three ways gives a third of the dose each.
- **Oranchuk et al. 2019** (*Scand J Med Sci Sports* 29:484-503, 26 outputs): long muscle length →
  hypertrophy 0.86-1.69%/wk vs short 0.08-0.83%/wk; **≥70% MVC required to change tendon structure**;
  ballistic intent has the higher ceiling for RFD, sustained the higher floor.
- **Bohm et al. 2015** (37 studies): tendon stiffness SMD 0.70; depends on **intensity, NOT
  contraction type**; **plyometric loading did NOT work** (too short for mechanotransduction);
  **>12 weeks needed**. Every climbing hangboard trial is 4-10 weeks — i.e. **shorter than the window
  in which tendon adaptation is detectable.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4532714/

## The variety-vs-boredom question — this is the strongest evidence in the whole pass
- **Variety materially improves ADHERENCE. [strong: RCT]** Sylvester et al. 2016, *J Behav Med*
  39(2):214-224. **N=121 analysed**, randomised, **participants blinded**, 6 weeks, 3×/wk,
  **volume and relative intensity held IDENTICAL** (160 reps/session). High-variety = 8 exercises ×
  2 sets rotating; low-variety = same 4 exercises × 4 sets every session.
  **Adherence 64.22% vs 50.89%** (11.56 vs 9.16 of 18 sessions), F(1,116)=5.55, **p=.02, ηp²=.05**.
  Manipulation check: shifted perceived VARIETY (β=.47) but not competence, relatedness or autonomy
  — variety is psychologically distinct from autonomy. **Full mediation** via perceived variety
  (indirect β=.16, p<.01; direct effect ns). Replicates Glaros & Janelle 2001 (63% vs 54%).
  https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/Variety_support_and_exercise_adherence_behavior.pdf
- **Structured variety does NOT cost strength; random churn does. [moderate]** Fonseca et al. 2014
  (*JSCR*, n=49, 12wk): title is the finding — "Changes in Exercises Are More Effective Than in
  Loading Schemes to Improve Muscle Strength." Varied exercise (squat+leg press+deadlift+lunge)
  improved **squat 1RM MORE than squat-only**, and hypertrophied all four quadriceps heads.
  Baz-Valle 2019 (*PLoS One*, trained men, RANDOM exercise selection): small impairment in rectus
  femoris only; everything else similar. Kassiano et al. 2022 (*JSCR* 36(6), 8 studies, 241 men):
  "**some degree of systematic variation seems to enhance** regional hypertrophic adaptations and
  maximize dynamic strength, **whereas excessive, random variation may compromise** muscular gains."
  => **Fix the SLOT, rotate the FILL.** Never rotate the thing you measure progress on.
- **Enjoyment predicts long-term adherence. [strong]** Teixeira et al. 2012 (*IJBNPA*, 66 studies):
  identified regulation predicts short-term adoption, **intrinsic motivation predicts long-term
  adherence**. Rodrigues 2020: enjoyment carries the largest effect on persistence; dropout
  concentrates in the first months.
- **Lattice already productised this** ("Can I choose my preferred way to train on a LatticePlan?"):
  **"A perfectly prescribed workout that you skip is infinitely worse than a slightly less optimal
  workout that you actually complete."** and "Your preferences are a strong bias, not a hard
  mandate." Their mechanism = **bounded choice within a fixed slot**, across four paired domains
  (Strength: board vs bouldering; PE; Endurance; **Finger Strength: hangboard vs lifting edge**).
  This is exactly the pattern Passion should copy.
- **Counterweight, kept for honesty — Bechtel:** "True endurance is built one long and boring session
  at a time, often over several years," and what separated athletes was "they were just consistent
  for longer." Reconciliation: consistency is the target, enjoyment is the MEANS to consistency —
  but some qualities do require tolerating tedium.
- **Coach tactics that converge:** Dan John (rotate within a pattern on ~2-week blocks);
  Nuckols/SBS (**tiered** core lifts / auxiliary / accessories — vary at the right level, and he
  treats monotony and tissue overuse as the same problem); Alex Bromley's **SRN** (Specificity /
  Recovery / Novelty) — "Show me the best prescription of sets, reps, and exercises and I will show
  you someone who got stale doing it"; Tuchscherer (RPE autoregulation as engagement);
  Barbell Medicine ("the *best* means is the one which that particular individual enjoys").
- **Gap:** nobody has run an adherence trial in CLIMBERS. Sylvester was inactive students. Flag it.

## Coach positions worth not re-researching
- **Hörst vs Steven Low is a head-on contradiction on max hangs.** Hörst: "Low-volume maximum-weight
  hangboard protocol is most effective at increasing finger flexor strength." Low: "the volume for
  max hangs is **insufficient** to generate good amounts of muscular hypertrophy" and repeaters at
  3-5 sets "are **superior for both strength and hypertrophy** compared to max hangs up to 9 sets."
  Both cite López-Rivera. Low's argument is **borrowed general hypertrophy literature** applied to an
  isometric finger task by analogy — flag that.
- **Hörst's protocols:** Minimum Edge 12s ×5 ×2 sets, exactly 3min rest, RPE 9-9.5. Max Weight 10s
  ×5/set. **7-53** = 7s hang, **exactly 53s rest**, 3 hangs/set, ≤5 sets, 2×/wk on a bouldering day —
  and "**Do not use this hangboard protocol during a period of performance climbing.**"
- **Dave MacLeod is NOT anti-strength-training** (common misreading). He built 9a on a hangboard,
  programs antagonist press-ups, and sells a hangboard. His actual contrarianism is narrower:
  (a) protocol minutiae are over-specified relative to effect, (b) normative finger-strength
  benchmarks are misused (Vlog #13 targets exactly this — a direct shot at Lattice-style
  benchmarking). Most of his content is video-gated; key quotes UNVERIFIED.
- **Tyler Nelson's published material is protocol-light and citation-free.** His blog gives
  overcoming vs yielding isometric TESTS (~30% force difference), load calc at 85% of measured 1RM,
  a Tindeq warm-up of 3s on / 5s off × 4 reps × 3 sets, then "just two reps" at max — and
  "**For finger strength training, less is more.**" No sets/reps/frequency for training, and
  **no scientific citations on any of the pages I fetched.** His wall-crawl / active-curl case study
  reports transfer to passive hangs but quantifies nothing. Treat as clinical reasoning.
  His one research-cited page (wrist position, citing Fischer et al. 2024): full crimp = 28-37°
  wrist extension, half crimp 14-17°, open hand 4-10°; "wrist extension + full crimp = maximum
  system tension." Grade: [his clinical opinion] unless a citation is visible.
- **Bechtel on pull-ups:** "Pull-Ups Are A Waste of Time" — the limiter is rarely lat/back strength.
  He still programs pulling; he objects to pull-ups as *the* answer.
- **Hampton, the line to remember:** "Climbers will dedicate years to getting 'strong enough' when
  what they really need is to learn how to **apply the strength they already have**." Also: "when
  done poorly, strength training can be used as a crutch to replace those foundational pillars."
- **Hamstring/heel-hook is the ONE place mechanism + tissue + injury pattern line up.** Ehiogu,
  Stephens, Jones & Schöffl 2020, *Wilderness Environ Med*: climbing-specific variant is "isometric
  muscle tension in extensive hip flexion with a flexed knee"; **biceps femoris most commonly
  injured**, attributed to heel-hooking. Plus a 17-patient case series. **No prevention trial.**
- **Copenhagen/adductor work is well evidenced — in FOOTBALL.** Harøy et al. 2019, *BJSM*, cluster
  RCT, 35 teams / 652 players: groin problem prevalence **13.5% vs 21.3%** (~30-41% reduction).
  Transfer to climbing is an assumption.
- **Steep terrain IS a core session.** EMG: trunk activation (rectus abdominis, obliques, erector
  spinae) rises significantly at **≥10-12° overhang** vs vertical, and oblique activation rises
  specifically when **one hand is released**. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24963738/
- **Jared Vagy / The Climbing Doctor:** real credentials, sound anatomy, well-organised protocols,
  but **confidence language consistently outruns the evidence** — "A Proven Injury Prevention…
  System" has no controlled trial behind it. Strongest on rehab, weakest on prehab. Hooper is the
  more careful source when you want climbing physio content.
- **Hooper's Beta cite literature on CONCEPT pages, and almost nothing on ROUTINE pages.** Don't
  use them as a blanket evidence proxy.

## Structural caveats to carry into every future consult
Median n ≈ 20-30. Interventions 4-10 weeks (shorter than the >12wk tendon window). Median PEDro
6/10, **no blinding anywhere**. Outcome is usually **self-reported grade**. Women ~25% of the pooled
sample. And the productive Norwegian group (Stien / Saeterbakken / Andersen / Hermans) authored a
large share of the trials **and** the meta-analysis of those trials — apparent replication is less
independent than the citation count implies.

## On-wall / on-board strength (added after the deep on-wall pass)
**There is NO peer-reviewed training-intervention study on ANY on-wall strength method.** Not one.
The entire peer-reviewed footprint of campus/board work is two acute post-activation-potentiation
studies and one youth injury-awareness survey. Everything else is coach opinion.

- **Nelson's on-wall isometric protocol (the real numbers):** set 45-50s, hold 4-7s per move,
  6-12 moves/set, **5-10 sets, 3-4 min rest**, session 15-40 min, **2-3×/wk, 3-4 week block**;
  progress by more moves → smaller edges → more sets. "It's not about power and making hard moves
  but about holding fast."
  https://strengthclimbing.com/dr-tyler-nelsons-new-active-finger-strength-training-protocols/
  => This is functionally **Hörst's H.I.T. with time-under-tension substituted for added weight**.
  **Lowest-risk on-wall finger method** — no impact, no added mass, self-limiting. The one to give an
  injury-cautious or first-year climber. Watch cumulative volume (10 × 50s ≈ 8 min near-max/session).
- **Hörst H.I.T.** (the historical weighted-on-wall protocol, and still irreplaceable — no LED board
  repeats one grip for 10-20 moves to failure): 45-55° past vertical, five isolated grips, failure in
  **10-20 hand moves / 15-30s**, **≤3 sets per grip**, **rest exactly 3 min**, ~2×/wk. Load went
  5-15 lb → 60 lb over **three-plus years**. Gated behind years of tendon base; mandatory taping.
- **Board ANGLE is the on-wall equivalent of adding plates** — the cleanest progressive-overload
  lever available. Shallower + smaller holds = finger bias; steeper + bigger = power/tension bias.
  (Relevant: the owner's cycle-1 call to keep TB2 at ONE angle preserves grade comparability, so
  angle is a *next-cycle* lever, not a within-cycle one.)
- **Campus board: highest-risk item, and the only one with epidemiological support for that.**
  Youth survey (peer-reviewed): **76.2% of adolescent competition climbers trained on the campus
  board**, but only **5.7% knew the correct safe starting age for double dynos**; most wrongly
  believed "13 and older" — precisely when growth-plate injuries peak.
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7037851/
  Hörst gates double dynos behind **V9 / 5.13 + five years**. Folk rule "V7/7a+" is the median of
  published advice. Nate Drolet's argument for **smaller edges over bigger moves** (Tension Power
  Strips) is the sane modern position.
- **Weighted bouldering is the clearest fashion-over-evidence item in the whole field.** Everyone
  discusses it; **even its advocates publish no protocol.** Mechanically it compounds load AND catch
  impulse simultaneously, neither controlled. The one verifiable guidance says **"it is not advisable
  to climb with weight at the highest limit of your climbing grade"** and its best use is endurance
  laps well below limit. => Put added load in weighted pull-ups/hangs where 5RM has actual data.
- **The one genuinely useful role for weighted pull-ups is as a PRIMER, not a strength driver.**
  Two peer-reviewed PAP studies: one set of **5RM weighted pull-ups ~4 min before** a power effort
  improved IRCRA Power Slap **+6.5 cm (+6.8%), p<0.001, η²=0.24** (n=16), and in the follow-up
  (n=24) weighted pull-ups beat one-arm pull-ups decisively (distance d=0.68 plus power, velocity and
  force, vs distance-only d=0.41 for one-arm). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7108669/
  and https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9679189/
- **Limit bouldering does NOT replace hangboarding for finger strength.** Medernach 2015 (n=23,
  randomised, 4wk): fingerboard grip strength **+2.5±1.4kg p<0.001**; bouldering **+1.4±2.8kg,
  p=0.109 (ns)**. Crimpd n=526: "Climbing Only" **p>0.99, no change**. Saeterbakken 2024 is the one
  study where bouldering alone did produce a small gain (+5.3%, p=0.048). => **Complements, additive
  — not substitutes.** But Hörst's claim that limit bouldering gives "the greatest gains in
  functional strength and power on the wall" is unfalsified as stated.
  Hörst's limit-bouldering rules worth keeping: 3-5 moves at absolute max, one to two grades above
  projecting grade, **"Fatigue has no place in limit bouldering"**, never two days in a row, ≤2×/wk,
  stop "as soon as it [quality] starts to noticeably dip."
- **One-arm hangs: excellent BENCHMARK, poor on-wall METHOD.** Every published protocol is on a
  hangboard, with a pulley. Finger risk is self-limiting (bodyweight caps load); **shoulder risk is
  not** — hence the 5-7s cap. On-wall one-arm hanging adds uncontrolled rotation with no way to
  scale. Verdict: a stunt, not a method.
- **Heel hook / hamstring is the ONE place peer-reviewed literature is AHEAD of the coaching.**
  Ehiogu, Stephens, Jones & Schöffl 2020, *Wilderness Environ Med*: heel hooking needs
  **significant CONCENTRIC hamstring activity**, so the authors question whether "current eccentric
  loading programs alone are appropriate" — i.e. **the Nordic-curl protocol most climbers copy from
  general S&C may be the wrong specificity.** Their phases: isometric across ranges → **concentric**
  full-range loaded → high-load unilateral **with tibial rotation** + ballistic. Return criteria
  include **single-leg hamstring bridge ≥30 reps**. Biceps femoris is the muscle (Barnes).
  https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1016/j.wem.2020.07.002
- **Copenhagen planks are the best-evidenced exercise in the entire strength brief — from football.**
  Serner 2013 (highest adductor longus activation of 8 exercises, plus highest external oblique and
  rectus abdominis); Ishoi 2016 (n=24, 2×/wk × 8wk → **+35.7% eccentric hip adduction strength**);
  Harøy 2018/2019 cluster RCT (**~41% reduction in groin problem prevalence/risk**). Dose: build to
  1 set × 12-15/side, 2-3×/wk preseason, 1×/wk in season. Closed-chain, so arguably more
  climbing-specific than most accessories. **Most neglected, best ROI item found.**
- **System boards are largely superseded hardware** (Tension Board does symmetry better and cheaper)
  **but H.I.T.'s grip-isolation-to-failure stimulus is NOT replicated by any LED board.** Could not
  verify the Vertical World / Verm / Yaniro lineage from primary sources — CBJ's history of training
  boards doesn't mention them at all.
- **The evidence gradient runs BACKWARDS to the fashion gradient.** Most fashionable (weighted
  bouldering, campusing) = least support + most documented harm. Least fashionable (Copenhagen /
  hamstring work, H.I.T.) = most support + clearest protocols.
- **"Specific" and "effective" are not synonyms.** Saeterbakken 2024 is the cleanest demonstration:
  strength gain real and large (ES 1.83), performance gain nil (p=0.43). **Specificity buys
  expression, not adaptation** — which is why on-wall and off-wall work keep testing as complements.
